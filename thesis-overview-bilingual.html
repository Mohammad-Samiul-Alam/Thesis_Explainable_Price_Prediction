Abstract

Online prices for the same kind of electronics differ widely across sellers, platforms, discounts and warranties, so buyers cannot easily tell whether a price is reasonable. This thesis predicts the price of consumer electronics listed on Daraz and Pickaboo from product information such as brand, category, platform, warranty and description. It compares Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost, then uses SHAP to explain which features pushed each predicted price up or down.

  • Data: 6,504 scraped listings, about 5,958 usable after cleaning.
  • Task: regression on log(price), judged by MAE, RMSE and R².
  • Contribution: explainable price prediction for Bangladeshi e-commerce, plus a reusable cleaning pipeline.

What the data revealed

  • Duplicates31 duplicate URLs, mostly empty ghost rows, plus some repeated products.
  • platform_nameFilled only for Pickaboo. Derive it from the URL.
  • No category columnBuild it from product names. A rough keyword rule left 1,335 rows as "other", so the rules need work.
  • Brand overlaps platformDaraz is 92% "No Brand", Pickaboo 0%. Read SHAP results for brand and platform with care.
  • WarrantyEvery Daraz row says only "Return & Warranty", which is not real warranty information. Useful warranty data exists only on Pickaboo.
  • Rating is weakAbout 60% of products have no rating or a rating of 0, and rating_count and availability contain junk. Do not rely on rating as a main feature, and drop the reviews column.
  • Price is very skewedRange ৳2 to ৳5,99,000, median ৳1,371. Skewness is 8.7 on raw price and 0.49 on log price, so the log choice is backed by data.

Biggest risk: data leakage

Listed price and discounted price correlate at 0.98. Using listed_price or discount_percent as inputs to predict the discounted price makes the model cheat and gives a fake R².

Target = final price (discounted price, or listed price when there is no discount), with listed_price and discount_percent excluded from the inputs. The alternative: keep discount as a feature and make listed_price the target. Either way, justify the choice in the thesis.

Dataset vs earlier notes. The notes use a Samsung smartphone as the example, but the data is mostly accessories: about 1,156 earphones, 1,144 chargers and cables, 575 laptops, 489 keyboards and mice, and only 436 phones (rough counts). Describing the scope as "consumer electronics, accessories and devices" is safer than "smartphones" in the viva.

The process, step by step

  1. Collect the data

    Start from raw.csv: 6,504 rows, 13 columns, from Pickaboo (4,536) and Daraz (1,959). Nine rows are already broken.

  2. Clean the data

    Drop the 9 broken rows, the 506 rows with no price, and duplicate URLs and products. Turn ৳ 3,049 into 3049. Drop the reviews column. Result: about 5,958 rows (Pickaboo 4,058, Daraz 1,900).

  3. Engineer features

    Platform from the URL, category from the name, brand (rare brands grouped as Other), warranty months and type, description length, availability, and specs such as GB, RAM, mAh, watt and inch pulled out with regex.

  4. Define the target, avoid leakage

    Target is the final price. Exclude listed_price and discount_percent from the inputs. Then take log(price).

  5. Split the data

    80/20 train-test split with cross-validation. Duplicates of the same product stay on one side, so the test set is truly unseen.

  6. Train three models

    Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost, each with hyperparameter tuning.

  7. Evaluate the models

    MAE, RMSE and R² on both log scale and in taka, with a category-wise breakdown. Laptop prices dominate the taka error.

  8. Explain with SHAP

    TreeExplainer on the best tree model: a global summary plot, a few dependence plots and waterfall plots for 3-4 products. End with a Limitations section: these are listing prices, not transaction prices, and the data is one snapshot.

Models and how they are judged

Linear Regression

Finds simple, direct relationships. The baseline.

Random Forest

Averages many decision trees to make a prediction.

XGBoost

Boosting-based model that can capture complex relationships.

MAE

The average error in taka. Actual 30,000 and predicted 28,000 is an error of 2,000.

RMSE

Like MAE but punishes large mistakes more heavily.

R²

How much of the variation in price the model explains.

Early baseline

A quick sanity check, not a final result: brand, category, platform and description length, 80/20 split, log price.

The research gap

Much Bangladeshi e-commerce research focuses on customer reviews, sentiment or purchase behaviour. Price modelling for consumer electronics listings, and especially explainable price prediction, is explored far less. This thesis does not claim that no price prediction work exists.

I predict the price of consumer electronics on Daraz and Pickaboo using Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost, then use SHAP to explain how each feature shaped the prediction. The work is not only price prediction but explainable price prediction.
